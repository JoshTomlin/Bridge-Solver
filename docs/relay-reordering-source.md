# Reordered opener rebids

The [rebid reference tables](relay-system/reordered-rebids.md) give compact
bid/shape/range lookup; this review preserves source interpretation details.

The unchanged [Word original](system-sources/reordering-openers-rebid.docx) and
[searchable extract](system-sources/reordering-openers-rebid.txt) are retained
in the repository. `Rnnnn` counts body paragraphs, including blanks. This
document supplements the main notes for natural one-level suit responses:
1C–1D/1H, 1D–1H/1S and 1H–1S. It does not reorder the artificial 1C–1S family.
The July email still controls its revised Bourke/fourth-suit entries.

There are 23 new practice entries: five single-suit rebids without three-card
support, four single-suit 2NT rebids with support, four reverses, and ten
mini–mega splinters. Each starts after the displayed opener rebid; this is an
entry-scenario trainer, rather than an unrestricted opening-bid selector.

## Single-suiters without three-card major support

These show six-plus in the opened suit, no second four-card suit and zero–two
in responder's major (R0024–R0066).

| Prefix | Shortage ask | Minimum | Subminimum | Invitation | Strong six-card major | Request 3NT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1C–1D–2D | 2H | 2S | 2NT | 3C | 3D; 3H solid | 3S |
| 1C–1H–2D | 2H | 2S | 2NT | 3C | 3D; 3H solid | 3S |
| 1D–1H–2S | 2NT | 3C | — | 3D | 3H | 3S |
| 1D–1S–2H | 2S | 2NT | 3C | 3D | 3H | 3S |
| 1H–1S–3C | 3D | 3H | — | — | 3S | — |

The shortage alphabet is no/middle/high/low. The low-shortage answer splits
14–16 / 17–19 in the four minor-opening entries. The eight-entry scope of
R0108 does not extend this extra range split to the heart-opening entry.
No-shortage uses ordinary keycard; identified shortage uses void-first SKC.
Only asks omit reserved calls; answers count consecutive bids.

Minimum/subminimum choices use the printed HCP bands and return to opener's
suit. Invitations are 7–9; the bot's disclosed practice policy declines with
14–16 and accepts in 3NT with 17–19. The source also permits discretionary
slam cues, which are not compulsory bot judgements here. A natural six-card
major gets 3NT without suitable support, game with a lower-range fit, or a
control cue with an upper-range fit. Singleton ten or better is suitable.
The separate solid-major option is conservatively restricted to AKQJT9 or
better; later control choices use the existing cheapest-control practice policy.

R0066/R0103 allow a natural unbid minor to become the keycard suit when
opener has denied shortage there. The action requires five-plus cards in
responder's hand. The next relay step remains keycard in the already agreed
suit even when that call names the other minor. Alternative minor agreements
close after the first keycard ask. Direct final contracts remain available;
the trainer does not claim every natural continuation after every enquiry.

## Three-card support and strength escape

The four 1m–major-response–2NT entries show six-plus in the minor and exactly
three cards in the major. Clubs use 14–19, split 14–16 / 17–19; diamonds use
16–19, split 16–17 / 18–19. Diamond 14/15 three-card support belongs to the
separate 1NT support route (R0145).

After clubs, 3D agrees clubs; after diamonds, 3C agrees diamonds. The major
agreement asks are 3S for hearts and 3H for spades. These use the explicit
no/high/low tables in R0111–R0169, including the fourth strength answer.
With a major agreed, 3NT can itself be a keycard ask. With a minor agreed,
responder's 3NT is an offer to play.

While strength remains undisclosed, a normal relay answer landing on 3NT
with a minor agreed, or 4M with a major agreed, promises the lower range.
An upper-range hand with that same answer uses the first call beyond the
entire normal response ladder. Thus ordinary keycard uses its fifth reply
and SKC its sixth. Other answers retain the broad range. The resulting
strength constraint is public and affects compatible samples. Once strength
is known, subsequent ladders do not add another escape. Queen, specific-suit
and void-follow-up enquiries apply the same rule when they meet game.

Source regression examples include:

- R0113: 3S club keycard, 3NT lower / 4S upper, with the same 0/3 answer.
- R0126: 3NT spade keycard, 4S lower / 4NT upper, two keycards plus queen.
- R0137: 3C club SKC, 3NT lower / 4D upper, two without queen.
- R0151/R0178: 3S diamond SKC, 3NT lower / 4NT upper, spade void.
- R0157: 4D heart SKC, 4H lower / 5H upper, spade void.
- R0135 explicitly splits the last ordinary answer to 2NT as 3S lower /
  3NT upper despite 3S not being the usual playable-game trigger.

R0192 permits an undisclosed maximum to continue over a final signoff. That
is optional: this trainer's final-contract action still asks partner to pass.

The first three shortage answers are high/low/equal outside lengths, including
5422 no-shortage and equal two-shortage cases. Ordinary practice reverses are
16–22, five-plus in the opened suit, four-plus in the reverse suit, opened suit
at least as long, and no outside four-card suit. The cap and shape ordering
are disclosed practice restrictions. A known single shortage uses SKC; equal
outside lengths use ordinary keycard.

The fourth answer is reserved for the source's 14/15 upgrades: 1345, 3145 or
3415 respectively in the three club entries. At 16+ the same shape uses its
ordinary shortage reply. The next step asks ordinary keycard in the agreed
suit, bypassing 3NT; responder's major Kickback is separately available.
After 1C–1H–3C–3H–4D, 4H is to play, 4S heart Kickback and 4NT spade
Kickback (R0101).

## Mini–mega splinters

The two calls immediately below 3M are high/low shortages (R0006–R0021).
The main notes supply continuation policy in P1442–P1458. Practice uses
11–13 mini / 17–19 mega, exactly four major cards, five-plus in the opened
suit and one shortage. Mini passes responder's 3M; mega raises to game.
Over non-serious or serious cues mini signs off and mega follows the allowed
control-cue route. The alternate opener-takes-over keycard route, Exclusion
and extreme-shape upgrades remain excluded and are shown as limitations.

## Conflicts and interpretation

The original documents are never silently corrected. The implementation uses
the following auction-consistent interpretations:

- R0060 says diamond SKC after a heart single-suiter; the agreed suit is hearts.
- R0123 says hearts after responder's spade transfer; agreement is spades.
- Several no-shortage shape lists reverse the minor lengths or retain the
  other major's length. The implementation derives shapes from the opened
  six-card minor and the promised major length; e.g. R0165 becomes 3262.
- R0128 assigns three/four keycards to the same answer. The standard 0/3,
  1/4, 2/5 without/with queen alphabet is retained; the range escape duplicates
  the original category rather than changing its keycard count. Other example
  shorthand does not narrow a 1/4 or 0/3 answer to a false exact count.
- R0107 generally splits low shortage immediately, but R0120 explicitly gives
  4D low shortage across the broad range. That specific heart-agreement table
  is retained; range is disclosed later if an answer lands on game.
- Source boundary judgements such as (13)14+, minimum 0–6/7, solid suits and
  slam interest use the narrower disclosed practice policies above.
