# Club opening relay source map

For concise lookup use the [club/Dappleton tables](relay-system/club-and-dappleton.md).
This source map preserves wider context, including unimplemented branches.

This map records the first- and second-seat club-opening rules needed for the next relay families. The main distinction is who describes their hand: the `1C–1S` auctions often describe opener, whereas Dappleton after a balanced opening rebid normally describes responder. Some auctions change that role partway through. These transitions must be represented explicitly rather than treating every relay as an enquiry about opener.

The original `1-Dappleton-Collaboration-3-.docx` attachment was read directly from its Word XML. Its 2,091 body paragraphs include blank paragraphs; `Pnnnn` references below retain that numbering. The XML contains no tables or tracked insertions/deletions. Both the unchanged original and a reproducible indexed extract are now retained with the [system sources](system-sources/README.md).

The July email supersedes the Word document's Bourke and fourth-suit branches. Those branches are reviewed separately. Split-range raises, Horton Two enquiries and the later strong-club clarification are covered by the [advanced source review](relay-advanced-source.md). Historical Word wording below is retained as provenance; the later user clarification takes precedence.

## Notation and modelling rules

- Unparenthesized shapes are in **spades, hearts, diamonds, clubs** order. Parentheses indicate permutations only within the parenthesized positions.
- Preserve qualifications such as `(14)15–19(20)`, “minimum”, “solid”, and “some slam interest”. They are not exact HCP predicates. A trainer can use a disclosed narrower subset.
- `N`, `M`, `H`, `L`, and `E` in a shortage alphabet mean no, middle, high, low, and even shortage. Retain the alphabet stated for that auction. Where the source gives no response alphabet, do not manufacture one from another branch.
- Record opening suit, transferred major, agreed trump, asking seat, answering seat, force level, and previous puppet/transfer choices separately. Both players can describe shape in the same auction.
- Use disjunctions for mixed balanced/unbalanced rebids. A bid does not necessarily establish one continuous HCP/shape intersection.
- Derive the declarer from the first partnership bid of the eventual strain, including artificial bids.

## Opening and entry conditions

The first/second-seat `1C` opening includes `(14)15–19(20)` balanced/semi-balanced without a five-card major, any `9+` unbalanced club hand, and `12/13` long-club `(322)6`/`2227` hands excluded from the weak NT range. Unbalanced hands normally have five clubs and a singleton/void, with `4414` an explicit four-club exception. Some weak singleton-honour hands open 1NT instead. Six clubs with a five-card side suit opens the five-card suit at `9–12(13)`, but clubs at `(13)14+`. **P0246–P0249, P0533–P0535.**

The exceptional `(16)17(18)` four-card fit is shown by breaking out of the relay at the lowest available major bid after responder's next action. Responder can pass, bid game, or answer an `NMHL` shortage enquiry. After responder's 3S request for 3NT, the exceptional opener instead cues 4m; responder bids game or shows shortage while bypassing 4M. Simple and jump major raises have different balanced ranges and belong to the deferred split-range family. **P0526–P0530.**

### Both-major and quantitative continuations

- Heart route `…2D–2H`: South's 2S shows four spades; 2NT is a balanced quantitative hand. After 2S, North 2NT denies a major fit, 3m shows a five-card minor without a major fit, 3M agrees that major and optionally asks `NHL` shortage, while 4M can be a bad minimum. After quantitative 2NT, North 3m shows five cards/nonminimum, 3H agrees hearts, and 3NT is a minimum without a fit. P0517 further supplies minimum 4H and maximum 3S/4m cues. **P0472–P0475, P0517.**
- Spade route `…2D`: North chooses 2H/2S/2NT. After a major preference South bids game or shows `NHL` shortage with slam interest. After 2NT South's 3m is natural, three/four cards, with shortage in the other minor; 3H shows 5/5 majors and 3S shows six spades. North's step over the minor agrees it and asks SKC; North 3S after the 5/5 description agrees hearts and asks `HL` shortage. **P0476–P0479.**
- Heart route `1C–1D–1H–2H`: North 2S shows four spades, 3H shows `(16)17(18)` with four hearts, and 2NT denies a major fit. A major agreement allows South to answer `NHL` shortage or bid game. After 2NT, South 3m shows a four/five-card minor with slam interest; North's step agrees it and asks SKC. **P0480–P0482.**

### Range enquiry and invitation

The heart acceptance has the extra `1C–1D–1H–1S` range enquiry: North 1NT = 14/15; 2C = the upper balanced range. After 1NT, South passes with 7–9, bids 2C/2D to play a five-card minor, or uses the ordinary schedule from 2H upwards. After 2C, a game force is established and the ordinary schedule begins at 2D. **P0484–P0491.** The upper range is inconsistently called 16+ in P0265, 16–19(20) in P0460, and 16/17 in P0484; do not claim one exact range without resolving the preceding exceptional-fit context.

After a 2C–2D puppet, South's 2H is an invitation: five/six hearts in the heart route, four/five spades in the spade route. In hearts North passes with a minimum, bids 3H with a nonminimum three-card fit, or 3NT with a nonminimum and two/three-card fit. In spades North 2S is minimum/three-card fit, 2NT minimum/two-or-three, 3S nonminimum/three, and 3NT nonminimum/two-or-three. Those “2(3)” choices are judgement, not disjoint exact-length predicates. **P0492–P0501.**

### Exactly five-card minor transfers

After South's 2NT/3C, North normally accepts 3C/3D. With only four cards in South's major, the exception is North's `(16)17(18)` four-card major fit, shown with 3M. When South showed a five-card major, North may prefer 3M with a good three-card major fit and no four-card minor fit. **P0519.**

South then shows `HL` or `NHL` shortage. North's step after that answer asks SKC in the agreed major or shown minor. Omit 3NT when North accepted the minor transfer; the source does not impose that same exclusion when North agreed the major. **P0520.** The available shortage alphabet depends on the exact known shape: a five-major/five-minor hand cannot have no shortage, whereas a four-major/five-minor 5422 can.

After South's three-suited 3D, North agrees a fitting suit with a suitable hand or bids 3NT/4NT. When South has only four in the major, suit agreement asks whether the hand is 4144/1444 or the corresponding void 40(54)/04(54). The paragraph does not fully enumerate the response ladder or priorities between fitting suits. **P0522.**

After South's 3H solid-six-major description, North 3S asks shortage; 3NT is to play, though South may choose to describe shortage over it. **P0523–P0524.** “Solid” requires a partnership or training definition before compulsory generation.

Opener 2S instead shows six-plus decent clubs and asks the corresponding shortage/strength questions. ## Source issues and safe boundaries

1. **Strength is often contextual.** Parenthesized ranges overlap; minimum/nonminimum and fit quality are not determined by HCP alone.
2. **1C–1S–2C–2D is not a literal replay of the first rebid table.** The explicit balanced 2NT and unbalanced 3D meanings must override a naive mapping. Other strong unbalanced coverage, including certain diamond-shortage shapes, is not completely enumerated. P0421/P0430.
3. **P0423 has a role typo.** After South's 2NT preference, North is the player who must bid 3C, despite the sentence saying “responder”. Turn order resolves the typo without changing the bid meaning.
4. **P0425 has the same pronoun problem.** After South's five-diamond 3D, North must give the SKC answer or 3NT doubleton response.
5. **Baron response priorities are incomplete.** Four-plus clubs overlaps the explicit six-club shapes; some `(44)` minor holdings qualify for both ordinary minor answers. A training subset can avoid the overlap; a full engine needs the partnership priority.
6. **The two Baron families differ.** In 1C–1S the meaning of South's 3H/3S depends on North's answer. In Dappleton they always show six clubs/diamonds.
7. **Not every shortage ask supplies its complete answer alphabet.** Where a clause only says “Shortage Ask”, require a confirmed shared rule or limit the profile to an explicitly defined alphabet. No guessed no-shortage step.
8. **The heart range-enquiry upper bound varies between paragraphs.** Preserve the exact entry and disclose a restricted strength range instead of silently merging 16/17 with 16–19(20).
9. **Exceptional four-card major support can interrupt a balanced relay.** Avoid generating it until the escape and subsequent seat/trump changes are supported.
10. **Several calls request the other player to ask.** A bid that “asks opener to keycard” is not itself the same player's keycard enquiry. Do not add a fabricated answer to keep a fixed asking seat.

Profiles should identify their complete fixed lead-in, public training bounds, current asking/answering seats, and exact supported exit. Undefined choices should end with a named boundary. A source boundary is not completion of the partnership's entire relay system.
