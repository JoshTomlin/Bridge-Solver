P0041 Preamble
P0042 Playing Outback Acol is more about a style than adopting rules for a new system. I therefore recommend reading the pre-amble.
P0043 Ideally, I would be a mathematician so that the style that under-pins Outback Acol could be explained in a formal, and perhaps more convincing way. Instead, the system is built on an understanding of how the maths might work. The start point is spotting nonsense lurking behind accepted bridge bidding theory, conventions, and systems. I do not know how good Outback Acol is, but I do have a well-reasoned sense when other systems and styles are non-optimal, or bad.
P0044 The first nonsense thing to notice about mainstream bidding is an obsessive pursuit of precision. The constraints of a bridge auction do not seem to be properly considered, and while sometimes impressive precision can be achieved to obtain a constructive result, that is not the sole way that a bridge auction obtains value.
P0045 The thing to value in a bridge auction is efficiency. A bridge auction is a four handed landscape where your team competes for the time and space to exchange information with both the quality and quantity of information being valuable.
P0046 The easy and superficial way to judge the success of a bridge auction is to look at the constructive result that was achieved. An expert player is untroubled to look at two hands and conclude that the best final contract is X. The harder thing to visualise and appreciate is what impact it will have on your opponent’s auction when you take away time and space. Disturbing your opponent’s time and space is difficult to value and mainstream bidding does not adequately seek to define or understand that value beyond truisms like “it’s a bidder’s game” and “pre-empts work”.
P0047 Outback Acol is based on a belief that when properly balancing all the objectives of a bridge auction there is an understandable tendency in mainstream theory to over value the two-handed objectives and under value the four-handed objectives. The system therefore seeks to go back to a fundamental view and place a higher value on bidding when it can achieve a four-handed goal.
P0048 Outback Acol sacrifices precision to ensure that four-handed goals are achieved. The obvious things to value when you are seeking to achieve four-handed goals are opening the bidding and entering an auction your opponents have commenced, as often and as quickly as possible.
P0049 Outback Acol values opening the bidding more than other systems. The accuracy of the information exchanged suffers when you open the bidding more often and the first thing to give up when playing Outback Acol is that sense of precision. The base skill that every serious bridge player needs in a bridge auction is good judgement. Outback Acol requires great judgement, but that judgement will commonly occur in a broader field of possibilities than with other styles and systems.
P0050 There is no “safe place” in a bridge auction. No matter how clear a choice of bid may be, the monsters are always under the bed. Outback Acol is not bothered to “embrace the variance” and the systems view is that bad results arising from a lack of precision are nothing more than the normal expectation in the optimal range of results.
P0051 Opening the bidding more often creates a clearer understanding for what is defined when we choose to pass rather than open. An opening pass in Outback Acol is more tightly defined than for other systems and the lack of accuracy that occurs with a broader range of opening bid is compensated by the additional accuracy of “pass”. Outback Acol is obsessive about collecting value from a better definition of an opening pass.
P0052 Outback Acol enters an auction already commenced by our opponents more often than other styles or systems. Rather than place significant value on the risks associated with entering auctions, Outback Acol is focused on the four-handed objectives. Second seat after your opponents have opened the bidding is plainly the best opportunity to create a contested auction and capitalise on four-handed goals. Outback Acol all but ignores the risks attached to vulnerability and less precise constructive auctions to enter an auction in this position, believing that adequate compensation will accrue from realising four-handed value.
P0053 Pre-empting in Outback Acol is again valued from a destructive, or four-handed perspective and little value is given to a constructive goal. The common view will be that the pre-empting standards of Outback Acol are too extreme and that being punished with a penalty double will result in many disastrous results. The contrary view is that the pre-empting standards of Outback Acol will obtain optimal four-handed equity and in that sense are better balanced than the generally accepted range for pre-empting.
P0054 Pre-empting should always consider the range of hands that are pre-empting rather than one individual hand. Outback Acol believes that the true value of a hand in a pre-empting range cannot be properly determined by considering its individual value. The individual value of a hand in a pre-empting range might be negative but it does not follow that this hand should not be included in a range of hands that pre-empt. I call this concept “Shania” which is eclectic poker slang for a similar principle. In practice, Shania is reflected in the desire to maintain a broad range of hands in positions like pre-empting rather than consider the merits of an individual hand.
P0055 Ugly and disastrous results will occur when accepting the pre-empting ranges in Outback Acol. As with the other non-mainstream ranges adopted by Outback Acol this propensity is to be viewed as nothing more than the usual variance in the optimal range of results.
P0056 The different ranges for opening, overcalling, and pre-empting used by Outback Acol are easy to adopt in the sense that they are well defined in the notes. Avoiding these ranges whilst playing Outback Acol is to swim against the stream as the structure of the system, like any good system, is built upon those standards. The difficulty for a serious player who correctly submits to the system and style will be how to best deal with the large and unfamiliar landscape created.
P0057 Common feedback for a new Outback Acol player will include “we never invite”. The statement is untrue but reflects a clear understanding about the maths for inviting and a required adjustment from a mainstream view about invitational sequences when playing Outback Acol. Broader ranges for common bids do not increase the value for inviting and in most circumstances, a broader range decreases the value for an invitational sequence.
P0058 The broader ranges in Outback Acol cannot profitably be “resolved” by choosing an invitational sequence. This is especially true for the 9-13(14) 1st and 2nd seat 1NT opening. Vulnerability is a significant factor when considering to invite such that inviting when not vulnerable should almost never occur after the weak 1NT opening. The system agreement is always to invite “heavy” and accept “light” as this optimises the value of an invitational sequence.
P0059 A different way to argue for the Outback Acol view of inviting is to consider it a ruthless pursuit of positive expected value from all bids.
P0060 Another common refrain for a new Outback Acol player is, “we never sacrifice”. Again, this statement is untrue. The broader ranges used by Outback Acol provide less certainty and in that context the equity accrued by those openings will be mis-spent by acting over an opponent’s action which is a “guess”. Sacrificing has value as a goal in a bridge auction however the prospect that bidding over an opponent guessing as a sacrifice will be a phantom, is larger playing a system with bigger ranges than the mainstream.
P0061 When bidding with potential value as a sacrifice the common Outback Acol view is that the bid must also derive value from another source, such as some small potential that the contract makes or the sensible prospect that the opponents will compete too high given the tempo of the auction.
P0062 Competitive auctions in Outback Acol are also governed by the idea that precision should not be attempted. Competing at the 3 level is primarily determined by a consideration of the Law of Total Tricks and competing to 4m can only occur when that bid has some significant value as an invitational bid.
P0063 The range of possible bridge hands is so large as to be overwhelming. Outback Acol ignores sensitive approaches for valuing a given hand and slavishly uses a 9 high card point line for opening the bidding. When considering whether a hand is balanced or unbalanced (or semi-balanced) the system takes the simple view that a hand with no 0/1 shortage in a suit is balanced rather than relying on supposedly more precise evaluations.

P0069 1NT Opening in 1st and 2nd
P0070 In 1st and 2nd seat a 1NT opening is 9-13(14) but the strength will vary slightly based on vulnerability and which of 1st or 2nd seat. The given range of 9-13(14) high card points is accurate because it will never be 15 and only on rare occasions will it be 8 – it can always be 9 or 14.
P0071 The 1NT opening attempts to cover the field of weak balanced or semi-balanced opening hands and routinely includes (4333), (4432), 5-card minor (5332), and 5-card minor (5422) hands. Other shapes that open 1NT will always be in the lower range of 9-13(14), about 9-11. These shapes are 5-card major (5332) (5422), 6-card minor (6322), 6-card hearts (6322) and 7-card minor (7222).
P0072 Opening 1NT with a shortage is normal when the shortage is a singleton ace, king or queen and again, usually when the hand fits into the lower, 9-11 range.
P0073 When a hand contains an exceptional quality 5 card major suit in the 9-11 range it is preferred to open 1M, especially with spades.
P0074 Without attempting precision, where “4” is the strongest range and “1” is the weakest range the variation for opening 1NT based on vulnerability and seat is as follows,
P0075               Unfavourable        All                    Nil                   Favourable
P0076 1st          4                              2                      2                      1
P0077 2nd           4                              3                      3                      2
P0078 The total variation in strength of the 1NT opening based on vulnerability and seat is small. In position 4 the 1NT opening will include maybe 35% of balanced 14 counts compared to about 15% of balanced 14 counts in position 1. In position 1 the 1NT opening will include 95% of balanced 9 counts compared to about 75% of balanced 9 counts in position 4.
P0079 Opening 1NT with 14 high card points is rare. Such an opening will almost never contain a 5-card suit and will most commonly be a (4333) shape. The other primary evaluation used will be the number of king-based controls (ace=2, king=1). Given a range of 9-(13)14 the average controls for a 1NT opening will be about 4 so opening 1NT with a 14 count will usually be a hand containing 3 or less king-based controls.

P0197 The guidelines for opening a weak 1NT can be shaded in favour of opening 1NT to emphasis its frequency and/or destructive effect.  Examples of these situations:
P0198 1 – Very early in a match.
P0199 2 – During a match in which we have opened a weak 1NT a lot with good results.
P0200 3 – Against any opponent who does not like it when we open a weak 1NT.
P0201 4 – In an attempt to generate swings on the rare occasions this is a good strategy.
P0202 The guidelines for opening a weak 1NT should rarely be shaded in favour of not opening it, as to do so would interfere with partners opening range and judgement about correct level. The system for opening bids in third or fourth seat depends on opening 1NT in first and second seat when in the stated range to do so.
P0203 The only time it is appropriate to shade the guidelines in favour of not opening a weak 1NT is when we have a substantial lead late in an imp match and we are vulnerable.
P0205 1NT Continuations 1st and 2nd
P0206 Outback Acol uses simple Stayman after a 1NT opening but this will include a broad range of Junk Stayman hands and advanced run hands (defined as 0-7 (4333) or (4432) shapes). Using Stayman does not promise a 4-card major and is the only way to invite opener to bid 3NT with a balanced hand.
P0207 After a 2D response to Stayman, 2M is to play and a 2H bid is correctable if opener is 32(xx).
P0208 1NT-2C-2x-2NT is natural and invitational.
P0209 1NT-2C-2H-2S is natural and invitational although responder should not routinely use this sequence when holding 4 spades and an invitational hand, sometimes choosing 2NT rather than 2S to avoid the transaction cost.
P0210 1NT-2C-2x-3m is natural and mildly invitational and will always include a 4-card major, a 5+ card minor, and be unbalanced. This sequence will always show a responding hand that would bid game if opener had the right major but would otherwise prefer to play in 3m unless opener has a great hand in context to play game.
P0211 1NT-2C-2D-3M is Smolen showing a 4-card suit and 5 cards in the other major, game forcing. When opener intends to play 4M in responders 5 card suit they should cue-bid with a good hand for slam.
P0212 1NT-2C-2H-3S and 1NT-2C-2S-3H are slam interest hands agreeing the suit shown by opener. In both sequences opener should predominantly bid 3NT as non-serious and otherwise bid 4M or cue-bid with the bottom and top 15-20% of hands.
P0213 Outback Acol uses 2D and 2H over 1NT as a transfer to the corresponding major suit promising 5+ cards in the major unless responder is utilising an advanced running option with a 0-7 (4333), (4432) hand. When a transfer is used as an advanced run responder will usually have 4 cards in the major suit or in rare cases when not vulnerable, 2 cards.
P0214 1NT-2H-2S-3H shows 5+/5+ in the majors, game forcing, given that we play Smolen. A 3S continuation shows a decent+ hand to play in spades and 4m shows a decent+ hand for hearts and a cue bid, 3NT is to play.
P0215 A slam interest (5332) with a major suit can be shown either by bidding a quant 4NT when the major is hearts, 1NT-2D-2H-4NT, or when holding spades via what would usually be a heart splinter, 1NT-2H-2S-4H. 1NT-2H-2S-4NT is Kickback, 1NT-2H-2S-4H-4NT is to play.
P0216 A transfer to a major followed by a jump shift in a new suit is a splinter (except 1NT-2H-2S-4H as above) showing a 6+ card major and mild slam interest with a shortage. A hand with a 6+ major, a shortage, and serious slam interest will respond 3M to a 1NT opening.
P0217 Outback Acol only super accepts after a transfer to 2M when opener has a big maximum or a bad minimum with 4(5) card support. Maybe 60% of the time (the middle range), opener with 4(5) card support for responders assumed major, should simply accept the transfer.
P0218 The only super accept bid is 2NT. After a super accept, responder can bid the final contract or retransfer, or bid 3NT asking opener to bid 4M. Bidding step (3C) after a super accept asks opener to clarify his hand. Opener will either bid 3M with a minimum or non-serious with a maximum (opener should not cue-bid with a maximum).
P0219 A new suit after a transfer is natural and game forcing. We prefer to bid 3NT holding just a 4-card minor after a major suit transfer unless holding a serious slam try. A transfer and a new suit will therefore always have some small+ slam interest. After a transfer and a new suit opener will evaluate his hand for slam and respond using fast arrival with a new suit being a good hand and a cue for responders second suit. If opener bids 4 of responders second suit it shows a fit for both suits and a good hand for slam (the exception to that will be 1NT-2H-2S-3H-4H which shows a minimum hand wanting to play 4H’s as described above).
P0220 A 2S response to 1NT is Baron promising at least mild slam interest. Opener responds 2NT with any (4333) and 3C, 3D or 3H as the lower of 2 suits in a (4432) hand which might also be a (5422) hand. When opener has a (5332) major suit 1NT opening, it is described as a (4333). Step after one of these responses asks opener to bid his unknown suit or bid 3NT when step was openers unknown suit. A 3S response to Baron promises 5+ clubs and a 3NT response promises 5+ diamonds.
P0221 Opener should bid on after responder signs off in game in a Baron auction when they hold a maximum 1NT opener or excellent shape and values for the game responder has decided upon.
P0222 A 2NT response to a 1NT opening is a transfer to 3m with less than game values, there are no strong options. Opener simply bids 3C and passes a 3D correction from responder. In a competitive auction 2NT is used the same way however can be any suit to play at the 3 level.
P0223 A 3 of a suit response to 1NT is natural with at least mild slam interest. After a 3m response opener can bid 3NT with no support and a bad hand and a new suit is a cue bid showing at least a good hand and average support or a poorish hand and good support. Opener can also bid 4m or 5m with 0/1 keycards and great support but an otherwise poor hand.
P0224 After a 3M response to 1NT opener can bid non-serious, or 4M with a bad hand for slam, or cue bid a new suit with support and a good hand for slam.
P0225 1NT-3NT is to play as is any 4-level suit response to a 1NT opening.
P0226 1NT-4NT is the minors 5+/5+. A quant hand will prefer to start with 2S Baron and can then construct either a quant 4NT or slam invitational auction.
P0228 1NT in Competition and Bruce Redoubles.
P0229 Outback Acol uses an advanced running method after 1NT-(P). There are no firm rules governing how to run but all 4333 or 4432 hands in the 0-7 range should bid something other than pass. When the 1NT opening was in second seat this range can be lowered to 0-5/6. With a 5+ suit (usually a minor suit) in the 0-7 range responder can pass after 1NT-(P) and later run by simply bidding the 5-card suit.
P0230 After 1NT-(P)-P-(X) we “automatically” redouble with the rare exception being when we hold a poor 5/6 card suit and a 9 count for our 1NT opening. Opener can also bid a suit after 1NT-(P)-P-(X) with 9-11 hands when protecting a lead late in the match or against certain opponents. It is important Shania to nearly always redouble in this position.
P0231 When responder bids after opener’s automatic redouble it is assumed to be a single suited hand but it can also be an “impossible” advanced running hand like a (32)44.
P0232 After the automatic redouble by opener, or an immediate redouble by responder showing 8+, a double of a suit bid by the opponents is takeout and the next double, and subsequent doubles, are penalties (a variant of 1-2-3 doubles where the redouble is 1).
P0233 After 1NT-(X), XX is (7)8+ balanced or possibly more values and semi balanced. It is rare to XX with an unbalanced hand. A double of a suit bid by the opponents after this start is Takeout in a 1-2-3 structure. After 1NT-(X) “pass” asks opener to bid a 5+ card suit or alternatively, redouble. After opener’s redouble in this auction responder will notionally bid the lower of 2 suits. Opener can pass in the auction 1NT-(X)-P-(P), usually with a (4333) shape but it should be rare.
P0234 Responder will prefer to bid a 5+ suit after 1NT-(X) rather than pass and then show a 2 suited hand with a 5-card suit.
P0235 Outback Acol uses simple Lebensohl after a 1NT opening and in competition. This applies after the auction starts 1NT-(X) or any other 2 level interference, allowing responder to play 3x in their long suit. If responder has passed a 1NT opening a subsequent 2NT bid shows a 2 suited hand wishing to compete at the 3 level.
P0236 If Stayman is doubled Outback Acol responds 2M as if there had been no double. If opener does not have a 2M response it is normal to respond 2D. Pass and redouble show 4+ clubs with the natural meaning being reversed. A pass shows a great hand with (4)5+ great or good clubs and redouble shows 4+ decent clubs willing to stand a pass. When the auction is 1NT-(P)-2C-(X)-P-(P) a redouble is to play and all subsequent doubles are penalty. A 2D bid after 1NT-(P)-2C-(X) infers diamonds.
P0237 If a transfer over a 1NT opening is doubled Outback Acol responds as if there had been no double with the exception being to XX to show exactly 3 card support and a surprising maximum. Outback Acol never chooses “pass” after a transfer is doubled and will never super accept in these auctions.
P0238 Dealing with interference at the 2 level after a 1NT opening, responder or opener can double a bid that shows the suit bid as takeout. When the interference does not promise the suit bid, responder can double as “values” in a 1-2-3 structure. When opener doubles 2 level interference that does not promise the suit bid it shows a great (4)5+ holding in the suit.
P0239 Dealing with interference at the 3 level after a 1NT opening, responder or opener can double as takeout. This takeout double will however always be based on a willingness to defend when partner has a suitable hand.
P0240 When 1NT is doubled in the immediate seat responder has 2 ways to play in 3 of a suit, either by bidding 3x immediately or via 2NT as Lebensohl. When responder chooses Lebensohl in these sequences it shows a greater willingness to defend a game contract and an immediate 3 level bid shows extra shape and some willingness to sacrifice.
P0241 Competitive auctions after a 1st or 2nd seat 1NT opening are difficult to judge given the large range of that opening. Responder should choose to either target game or part score accepting that big range and avoiding an invitation. When responder shows values with an initial double in these auctions and then competes to the 3 level it is assumed to be mildly invitational. When responder bids freely at the 3 level rather than using Lebensohl it is choice of game.
P0242 Complying with the 1-2-3 structure to show a takeout double is not mandatory when opener or responder has a bad minimum type hand. Choosing to penalise the opponent’s interference in these auctions is largely based on the holding of a “trump surprise” and doubles based on high cards should generally be avoided.
P0243 2NT, other than being defined as Lebensohl, is always scrambling, 2 places to play, in a competitive 1NT auction.
P0245 1C Opening 1st and 2nd
P0246 The 1st and 2nd seat 1C opening is any (14)15-19(20) balanced/semi-balanced hand without a 5+ major suit, any 9+ unbalanced hand with clubs, and 12/13 (322)6/2227 hands which are not included in the opening 1NT range.
P0247 The unbalanced range for a 1C opening includes a 4414 shape but otherwise will show 5+ clubs and will have a 0/1 shortage unless 12+ (322)6, 2227.
P0248 The objective of this over-loaded 1C opening is primarily to control the level of a final contract when opener has a 14+ balanced hand. The transfer/relay continuations after a 1C opening embed both the (14)15-17, and 18/19(20) range of balanced hands, at or below, 1NT.
P0249 In addition to enabling accuracy at a low level regarding best strain and game/slam contracts another advantage is the ability to respond to a 1C opening holding sub-minimum hands given that opener will rarely be jumping to a high level with their rebid. The 1st and 2nd seat 1C opening is forcing although it does not “protect” any game forcing hands and responder can choose pass with 5+ clubs 0-3/4 albeit that a 1NT response is generally preferred.
P0250 The responses to a 1st or 2nd seat 1C opening are,
P0251 1C-1D shows 4+H’s.
P0252 1C-1H shows 4+S’s.
P0253 1C-1S shows (7)8+ usually without a 4-card major suit.
P0254 1C-1NT shows 0-6(7) usually without a good 4-card major suit.
P0255 1C-2C shows 10+, with 6+D’s single suited.
P0256 1C-2D/H/S shows 6(7) in the suit bid 0-6(7).
P0257 1C-2NT does not exist.
P0258 1C-3C shows 6+C’s 3-6(7).
P0259 1C-3D/H/S shows (6)7 in the suit bid 0-6(7).
P0260 1C-1D
P0261 Responder holding 4/4 in the majors should respond 1D rather than 1H to a 1C opening. The 1D response includes hands with 4H’s and a longer minor.
P0262 Continuations after 1C-1D are as follows,
P0263 1H – 14-17 balanced with 2-4 H’s, if holding 4H’s the range will be (16)17(18)
P0264 1C-1D-1H – Pass will always be a 5-card heart suit and 0-7.
P0265 1C-1D-1H - 1S is a range enquiry, opener bids - 1NT with 14/15 balanced - 2C with 16+ balanced.

P0529 The (16)17(18) range is intended to be a narrow band of hands catering for the sub-minimum responsive hands as well as potentially providing clarity in slam going auctions. As usual, OA is concerned to avoid playing 3M in an uncontested auction.
P0530 To show the (16)17(18) 4 card support hand opener simply breaks out of the relay/transfer structure after responder’s next bid by bidding the major at the lowest level. When opener has shown the (16)17(18) 4 card support hand by bidding 2M or 3M responder can either pass or bid game in the major or respond to a Shortage Ask NMHL. When responder has bid 3S over the initial 1 level transfer as a transfer to 3NT and opener has the (16)17(18) 4 card support hand they should cue-bid 4m and responder should either bid 4M or respond to a Shortage Ask bypassing 4M.

P1060 Horton Two’s
P1061 2D/H/S openings in 1st and 2nd seat promise 4+ spades as well as 4+ in the suit opened, or in the case of a 2S opening, clubs. The strength of a Horton Two (HT) is defined as any hand that is less than a normal 1 level opening so nearly always 0-8 high card points.
P1062 A hand containing 6+ spades prefers to open 3S or 4S and therefore in a HT, spades will always be 4 or 5 cards in length with an exception when vulnerable and holding a poorish 6 card suit. The length of the second suit in a HT is 4+.
P1063 The high card points in a HT can range into a 9 count with concentrated values in the 2 suits but only when vulnerable.
P1064 A HT should be opened with any qualifying shape except in some conditions when the shape is 4(432). When holding a 4(432) and vulnerable, a HT opening is a pass unless it has good quality suits.
P1065 (4441) and (5440) shapes can provide a choice of which HT to open. The first choice is always to show the major suits by opening 2H. Choosing between 2D and 2S it is preferred to open 2D but the decision also depends on suit quality.
P1066 Continuations after a HT are dependent on which HT is opened but there are some consistent rules.
P1067 Simple support of a major suit at the 3 level shows a wide range of hands including weak shapely hands and up to balanced hands of about 15 high card points. A raise of a major suit is defined as mildly invitational and the extent of the invitation is partly dependent on vulnerability. When vulnerable a simple raise of a major suit by responder can be raised to game with less than when not vulnerable but in either case a raise to game will commonly include extra trumps, shape and good high cards compared to a median HT.
P1068 A simple bid of a new suit after a HT opening is natural, to play. Opener may raise a new suit but only if they hold support and extra shape/high card values as well.
P1069 A jump bid in a new suit shows a great suit and is forcing, setting trumps. Bids after a jump bid in a new suit by responder are cue bids. This does not apply for a 2S opening when responder cannot jump in a new suit naturally below game.
P1070 Kickback for hearts (4S) is not available after a 2H opening and 2H-5C is substituted as Kickback for hearts. The auction 2D-4H is Kickback and to play 4H responder must proceed through 2D-3H.
P1071 Opener may not Kickback after a HT opening.

P1293 Competition
P1294 Competitive HT auctions are simplified by responder always being in control. Opener in a competitive auction will always pass unless they have exceptional shape (likewise opener may raise a non-forcing bid by responder but only with exceptional shape).
P1295 When there is immediate interference over a HT opening, 2NT retains its meaning as an enquiry as do all other bids. Double by responder is always penalties and opener may double with more defence than the median hand after responder has doubled.
P1296 When there is interference after responder has used the 2NT enquiry opener should answer the enquiry when the interference is 3S or less in the same step order as if there had been no interference. P1297 In the event of higher interference than 3S opener should pass with a minimum and otherwise find the most convenient and descriptive bid with the default choice being double.

P1431 3NT Opening
P1432 In 1st and 2nd seat a 3NT opening shows 5 spades, 6/7 hearts 9-13. A 4m continuation agrees the corresponding major suit and asks opener to cue-bid with a decent+ hand, otherwise bid 4M.
P1433 In 3rd seat a 3NT opening is a wide range of strong 15+ hands commonly based on a long minor. The option to open 3NT in 3rd seat will consider partners pass and may include 5 spades.
P1435 3 and 4 Level Openings 1st and 2nd
P1436 A hand with a 6+ suit and 0-8 high cards with the opportunity to open the bidding should open 3 or 4 of the suit. The exceptions to this broad range of pre-empts can be when holding a (6322) shape vulnerable, as well as some 3361, 3316 hands given the ability to support partners major suit.
P1437 Opening at the 3 level will never be 9+ as those hands should open at the 1 level. Opening 4M will routinely include hands up to about 12/13 points. The principle for this is simply that a 4M pre-empt is effective and a large range should be maintained. A 4M pre-empt will usually be a hand with a “promising” shape and include 6/5 shapes and 6/4 shapes. The 3M pre-empts will never be a hand with a “promising” shape unless at the very lowest end of the 0-8 strength range.
P1438 A 4m pre-empt is defined as a hand that is very unlikely to be suitable in a 3NT contract. In practice this will normally be a 7+ suit of poor quality and/or extreme shape.
P1439 A new suit after a 3 level pre-empt is natural and forcing. Opener should usually bid 3NT without 2+ support for partner’s suit.
P1441 Mini Mega Maxi Splinters
P1442 A single or double jump shift by opener is usually a mini/mega/maxi splinter promising 4 card support for responders 1x major response to a 1 level opening and 0/1 cards in the suit bid.
P1443 The mini range is better than an absolute minimum opening hand given that OA opens every unbalanced 9 high card point hand. The absolute minimum splinter hands will be a simple raise followed by the range relay described elsewhere in these notes. The mini splinter range is about 11-13 but might include good 10 high card point hands and some poorish 14 high card point hands.
P1444 The maxi range is the next range of strength, above a mini range, and is defined as a hand that is strong enough to force to game or at least has an expectation that if responder has a minimum hand, game will still be a sensible/appropriate chance of making. An approximate high card point range for a maxi splinter is 14-16 but can be 13 or 17 depending on the shape and quality of values.
P1445 The mega range is the strongest range and will commonly be about 17-19 high card points.

P1689 Serious/Non-Serious
P1690 Once an auction has agreed a major suit fit and is game forcing at or below 3M, Serious/Non-Serious continuations usually apply. When the agreed major is hearts 3S is defined as non-serious and 3NT is defined as serious and a spade cue-bid. When the agreed major is spades 3NT is defined as non-serious.
P1691 The Serious/Non-Serious auctions rely on the player armed with the convention to bid 4M with a terrible hand for slam or avoid the non-serious bid with a great hand for slam that usually wants to take control of the auction by cue-bidding another suit. In general terms it is expected that the choice of non-serious will be made with a big majority of hands, say 65-70% and that of the remaining hands in each range, half will bid 4M or cue-bid.

P1846 2nd Seat (1X) Overcalling
P1847 Outback Acol overcalls in 2nd seat whenever there may be some value, primarily focused on 4 handed value. The range of a 2nd seat overcall is only limited at the top end of the range such that it is undesirable to make a simple overcall of a (1X) opening in 2nd seat with a 14 count and should almost never be done with a 15 count.
P1848 No significant distinction for strength or suit quality is made for the difference between a 1 level overcall and a 2 level overcall. When overcalling with the lowest end of the strength range the relevant factors in deciding to overcall are the likelihood and desirability of directing a lead, as well as the 4 handed value of a specific overcall.
P1849 Some simple overcalls have more 4 handed value than others. Examples of good value simple overcalls are (1C)-1S or (1D)-2C. In addition to taking high value space from opponents these overcalling positions should not have a high susceptibility to directing a lead and should be made on poor suits. The value for these overcalls commonly derives from exploiting the wide-ranging nature of an opponent’s 1m opening.
P1850 Overcalls with low 4 handed value include (1C)-1D and (1S)-2C. In these auctions there is a greater likelihood to directing a lead and an overcall should have a decent suit or be an opening strength type hand. When overcalling in the auction (1C)-1D it can be assumed that the overcall has at least marginal lead directing value as otherwise the overcall would not have sufficient value.
P1851 Continuations after a simple overcall in 2nd seat are based on partner’s 4 handed view. A common experience is for partner in 4th seat to have an excellent hand and hear the following auction, (1X)-O/C-(bid). In this position it will be clear that the over-caller has marginal or no significant values and 4th seat should optimise the available 4 handed value available. A simple raise of a simple overcall can therefore be made on a very wide strength range of hands with support.
P1852 A new suit from 4th seat after a 2nd seat overcall is constructive and forcing whenever the over-caller has 3+ card support or when the over-caller has about opening hand strength. There are exceptions to 4th seat always having a constructive hand when bidding a new suit such as when it should be plain that the opponents have the balance of strength, for example, (1D)-1S-(2C)-2H. The 2H bid in this auction is likely based on a lead directing value and is a hand that can tolerate partner supporting hearts at a high level.
P1853 When 4th hand introduces a new suit after a second seat overcall the overcaller can only pass when they have 0-2 cards in the new suit and less than a maximum overcall strength so about 0-10. Showing support for a new suit should distinguish the overcallers strength if possible, by jump supporting with a 11-14 hand and a simple raise if 0-10.
P1854 NT bids by 4th hand after a second seat overcall show a good hand with game interest if the overcaller is in the 10-14 range. A 2NT bid by 4th hand after a 2 level second seat overcall should always contain support for the overcalled suit and will pass a correction to 3 of overcallers suit (when the overcaller has a 0-9(10) hand).

P1856 After (1X)-O/C-(bid) X is always doubleton support for the overcall suit and a good hand.

This hand may be an exception if say the auction proceeded P-(1D)-P-(1NT/2D)-P-(P). A takeout double would be reasonable given this hand is a near perfect shape and the low level of the auction.
P1884 Responding as a passed hand to partners 3rd seat overcall should also carefully consider the field created. The overcaller has denied an intermediate jump hand but can otherwise have a strong hand or a hand that is not strong but can tolerate partner bidding with support. When the 3rd seat overcall is 1M responder should avoid bidding 3M and only do so with 4 trumps and a shortage. Raising a 2m 3rd seat overcall to 3m can be done much more liberally (without 4 trumps and without great shape) as it has good disruptive effect given that our opponents may not yet have discovered their fit or properly expressed the strength of their fit.

P1891 Intermediate Jumps
P1892 Playing intermediate jumps is an important part of the overcalling structure in Outback Acol. The nature of an intermediate jump differs from the classical definition of that bid and it is shaped by position, vulnerability, and the principle that Outback Acol limits the top end strength of a simple (1X) overcall in 2nd seat. The other broad principle for an intermediate jump is that it will always want partner to bid game in its suit with marginal values and a fit.
P1893 Assuming the strength of an intermediate jump is rated from 1 as the weakest range, to 3 as the strongest range the respective range should be as follows,
P1894 Seat                       Not vulnerable                  Vulnerable
P1895 2st                                        1                                      2
P1896 3rd or 4th                             3                                       3
P1897 The range of an intermediate jump should remain constant as a strong hand, usually with a 6+ suit, but varying at the ends of the range in accordance with the above table.
P1898 An intermediate jump should usually contain sound defensive values. Without good defensive values a hand is more likely to be an appropriate jump to game or a pre-emptive double jump. Expressed as high card points the range of an intermediate jump is variable. Assuming a common shaped hand with a 6 card suit the usual range will be about, 1 = 14-16, 2 = 15-17, 3 = 16-18.
P1899 The suit shown by an intermediate jump will nearly always be a 6+ suit however, particularly in 2nd seat, an intermediate jump can be a great 5 card suit as an alternative to a simple overcall or doubling and bidding a suit.
P1900 Intermediate jumps also apply over weak 2 openings by our opponents. In this instance the strength of an intermediate jump will be a “3” based on the table above.

P1904 Leaping Michaels
P1905 Leaping Michaels does not require a detailed defence as double is retained as takeout. A takeout double in this instance will always promise defence and will often be passed. P2085 Carding
P2086 The carding agreements for Outback Acol are simply what I am used to and have never significantly deviated from.
P2087 Leads are 2nds and 4ths, low from an honour and top of a doubleton. Inherent in that description is 2nd from 4 small. Over-lead honour sequences and interior honour sequences except for an AK holding where it is ace for attitude and king for count. The first discard is designated as reverse attitude.
P2088 All signals are reverse (UDCA). Subsequent count signals are original count, not present count.
P2089 Suit preference signals are the default whenever it is utterly clear to the defence that it cannot be confused with the priorities mentioned above.
P2090 I am addicted to count even though there is evidence that this is not optimal. Similarly playing “2nds and 4ths” is preferred as easy for any good player and most familiar for me. My attitude towards carding agreements is to avoid dispute about the nature of a signal and therefore maintaining a simple agreement with a slavish approach.
