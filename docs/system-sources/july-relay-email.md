## Literal source rules

The following transcription preserves the email's substantive wording, including
role slips and abbreviated shape notation. Line wrapping and punctuation have
been normalized. Bid tables reproduce explicit bid assignments only.

### E1: scope and the base 1D–1NT relay

The base relay is **1D–1NT**, expanded into “all the 3 bid 1m auctions without a
systemic response,” replacing Bourke Relay and fourth-suit relay.

For **1D–1NT**, opener first describes range “with either 2C/D or by reversing.”
Responder then uses **2H over 2m as relay**. Alternatively, **2S over 2m shows
6+ solid clubs and asks for shortage**.

In response to the relay, opener stacks hands by:

1. Describing a second suit “in there rank order.”
2. Middle/high/low shortage.
3. Three-suited hands on top.

The exception is “the rare 12/13 (6322)2272 hand which doesn't open 1NT”; these
hands bid **3NT over the relay**, consistent with the other relays.

The email does **not** state which numeric range belongs to 2C or 2D. Any use of
2C = 12–15 and 2D = 9–11 must retain its separate earlier source/confirmation.

### E2: subsequent shape relays and agreeing trump

“The pattern is the same when responder reverses with step being the relay and
opener stacks high/low shortage and then the occasional 3 suited hand.”

When opener shows hearts by reversing or in
**1D–1NT–2m–2H–2S**, two three-suited types are possible. They are ordered by
high/low shortage. The email explicitly gives:

| Auction through the next answer | Shape |
|---|---|
| 1D–1NT–2m–2H–2S–2NT–3H | 1444 or 0454 |
| 1D–1NT–2m–2H–2S–2NT–3S | 4441 or 4450 |

The only other three-suited type is after opener shows four spades: **4144 or
4054**.

After **1D–1NT–2m–2H**, responder can no longer agree clubs unless opener shows
a non-club shortage. With solid clubs, responder should have used 2S instead of
2H.

When opener shows a second suit, **step+1 agrees that second suit** and is the
same further relay. When opener shows a non-club shortage, **step+1 agrees clubs**
and otherwise uses the same relay. The simple next-step continuation always
ostensibly agrees diamonds.

**3NT by responder is always to play.**

than a strictly game forcing hand.” If opener does not have the terrible hand,
the other hand types are stacked according to the entry auction, loosely

This weak-hand screen is introduced for the eight expanded entries. It is not
an instruction to insert another screen into the base 1D–1NT–2m–2H relay, whose

### E4: the four unlimited entries

The email calls the final four entries unlimited and warns that they have
special cases arising from other parts of the system.

**1C–1D–1S–2D**:

- With four spades and 12+ values, responder can use the relay when they lack a
  natural 3S/4S response to 1S. After receiving the stack response, step+1 agrees
  spades.

  shortage, then the rare three-card heart-support hand.
- Opener should have bid 2H over 1D with a 9–10(11) **4315** hand. With 12+,
  opener bids 1S and this type is stacked above the high/low shortage types.

**1D–1H–1S–2C**:

- Above the shortage responses come **4144/4054**, then the rare **4351, 16+**.
- With 9–15 and that heart-support pattern, opener would have bid either 2H

### E6: 6/5 postscript and general agreement order

The author says the omitted **6/5 hands are the last types stacked when they
apply**. The stated opening rule is to open “the higher ranking 5 card suit in
a 6/5 shape unless you have say 14+, or roughly reversing values.”

The **default relay always ostensibly agrees opener's minor**. Once opener has
shown a shape, responder can agree other available suits, in this order:

1. Opener's second suit.
2. Responder's suit, when a non-shortage is shown.
3. Opener's third suit.
4. The other minor, when responder has a surprise six-card minor after initially
   responding 1M to a 1m opening.

The author explicitly says that the steps depend on which choices are available.
The paragraph repeats: **3NT to play; otherwise responder is always in relay**.

The email ends by calling itself a quick condensed account that may be corrected
by Dave. None of the six images defines a Shortage Keycard response ladder,

Two likely role slips are preserved in the transcription: E2 says “responder
reverses” while immediately making opener the describing hand; E3 says the weak
screen lets “opener” use the relay on about 12 points although responder is the
asker. The surrounding auctions support treating these as wording slips, not as
instructions to swap the asking and answering players.

For shape predicates, high/low shortage is relative to the unshown suits after
the long suits are established. Thus in the explicit diamond/club tables the
ordinary high shortage is spades and low shortage is hearts. This mapping is a
bridge-ranking interpretation of the labels, not spelled out beside E5's rows.

The broader system's opening, transfer and rebid definitions still constrain
which hands can reach each entry. A relay branch must retain those entry facts;
for example, weak three-card support hands routed through a prior raise must not
reappear as unrestricted candidates in an unlimited two-suit rebid branch.
