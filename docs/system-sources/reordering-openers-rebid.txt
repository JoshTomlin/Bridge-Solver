R0001 Re-ordering Openers Rebid for Reversing, Mini-Mega Splinters, and 1/3 (not 3 card support) Rebid.
R0002 The purpose of this part of the system is to reorder bids to accommodate their frequency and utility efficiently. The applicable auctions are any auction with a 1 level response promising a natural suit, 1C-1D/1H, 1D-1H/S, and 1H-1S followed by a reverse, Mini-Mega Splinter, or 1/3 (not 3 card support rebid.
R0003 Mini-Mega splinters have little use for the bidding space between the splinter and 3M and are therefore re-ordered to the 2 bids prior to 3M following the high shortage/low shortage pattern. Continuations after a Mini-Mega splinter are dealt with in a separate chapter of the notes however responder will always bid either 3M, 4M, non-serious in the M, or serious (cue-bid) for the M.
R0004 The relatively frequent of the three types of rebid is to show (13)14+ and a 6+ suit (not 3 card support for responder’s major) and that has been re-ordered to the most economical position allowing responder more options to find the optimal final contract.
R0005 Reversing bids now have less space than with a “normal” ordering however responder will always have the option to sign off with a poor or sub-minimum initial response.

R0022 1/3 (not 3 card support for responder’s major) Rebid
R0023 The applicable auctions are,
R0024 1C-1D-2D, 1C-1H-2D, 1D-1H-2S, 1D-1S-2H, and 1H-1S-3C. These auctions all show a 6+ opening suit 14+ denying a second 4 card suit and 3 card support for responder’s major.
R0025 Responding to a 1C opening responder can have a sub minimum 0-4 hand unlike when responding to a 1D/1H opening when responder usually promises 5+ when bidding at the 1 level. The auctions starting with 1C always have an extra step available before reaching the 3 level in openers suit compared to the 1D/H auctions and this is used to show a sub minimum hand (1D-1S-2H also has an extra step available).
R0026 Continuations after the 1/3 (not 3 card support for responder’s major) rebid always start with step being a shortage ask, ostensibly agreeing openers suit. Step+1 shows a minimum hand, 5-6. Step+2 only applies after a 1C opening (plus 1D-1S-2H) and shows a sub minimum, 0-4. Returning to 3 of openers suit is always invitational, 7-9.
R0027 A continuation above 3 of openers suit includes 3S as a transfer for opener to play 3NT and 3NT to play (except for 1H-1S-3C when 3S shows 6+ spades). The remaining bids below 3NT show responders strong 6+ major. The 1C auctions have 2 steps available for responder to show his major whilst the 1D and 1S auctions only have 1 step available. The first or only step shows a 6+ major suit that will play as trumps opposite a singleton honour including a singleton 10. The remaining step in a 1C auction shows a solid 6+ major suit playable opposite a void.
R0028 A better way to describe this is the bids before 3 of openers suit and the bids after openers suit. The bids before 3 of openers suit are ordered from lowest to highest as 1 – shortage ask – minimums starting with 5/6 minimum then sub-minimum. The bids after 3 of openers suit are showing a 6 card major and 3S to bid 3NT and 3NT always to play – the priority is always to show a 6 card major and when there is room to show a decent 6 card major and then a solid 6 card major.

R0064 When responder has a 6+ major after opener shows a 1/3 rebid hand without 3 card-support they can proceed via the shortage ask and then bid their major naturally when opener denies a shortage. Responder can simply bid 4M over the 1/3 rebid when they have a suit playable opposite a void and a minimum hand otherwise.
R0065 When responder shows a solid 6+ major continuations are serious/non-serious. When responder shows a 6+ major playable opposite singleton 10 or better opener should either bid 3NT without a fit for the major or, when they have a fit for the major, bid 4M with a minimum and 4m with a maximum and a cue-bid.
R0066 Responder may have an unbid (5)6+ minor in these sequences and therefore bidding that minor after a response to the Shortage Ask (and opener denying a shortage in that minor) is Shortage Keycard in the presumed 6 card minor unless bidding the minor is step in which case that bid is retained as Shortage Keycard in openers minor.
R0067 Reverse’s
R0068 The applicable auctions are 1C-1D-2S, 1C-1H-2H showing a reverse into diamonds, 1C-1H-3C, 1D-1S-3C showing a reverse into hearts.
R0069 Step after a reverse into diamonds is always a shortage ask ostensibly agreeing clubs but can be based on a solid 6+ major suit. A 3D bid is also a shortage ask, agreeing diamonds. The bids in between step and 3D describe a minimum hand and when possible, a minimum 5/6, is step+1 and a sub-minimum 0-4 is step+2. A 3H bid is always a 6+ major looking for minimal support, a 3S bid is a request for opener to bid 3NT and 3NT is to play.
R0070 A reverse in hearts has significantly less room. The priorities are enabling responder to stop in 3 of openers minor by either passing or bidding 3m. The first available bid after denying a minimum is a shortage ask and 3H is a shortage ask agreeing hearts if available. A 3S bid is always a 6+ major looking for minimal support and 3NT is to play.

R0104 1/3 Rebid 14-16/17-19 Relay Distinction
R0105 When openers rebid shows a single suiter 1/3 rebid hand either with 3 card support for responder’s major (2NT rebid) or without 3 card support (as described above) the strength range is a broad 14-19. In auctions where responder chooses to relay the big strength range for opener creates a problem when openers response to a relay question lands on a playable game contract. The relevant contracts are 3NT when a minor has been agreed and 4M when a major has been agreed.
R0106 To resolve this issue opener should only respond to a relay landing on a playable game contract when they are in the 14-16 range. Otherwise, when opener is 17-19, they should utilise the first “impossible” response to the relay question to show a response that would have landed on a playable game contract but they are in the higher strength range.
R0107 In auctions where opener has the last response to the Shortage Ask, a low shortage, they should immediately describe their strength range by either choosing the ordinary low shortage step with 14-16 or bidding step with 17-19 (no “equal”, extra step to a Shortage Ask, applies in these auctions).
R0108 The distinction in range applies to the 8 initial auctions when the opening 2 bids are 1m-1M followed by opener describing a single suiter with or without 3 card support for responder’s major suit.
